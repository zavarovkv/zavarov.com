Good teams celebrate making a meaningful improvement to their company's business results. Bad teams celebrate managing to release any product at all.

A customer-focused culture.

Empowerment means teams have the right to solve the business problems assigned to them in the way they believe is best.

A good product manager knows that every product starts out “wrong,” because nobody can get everything right the first time.

If your company is large enough to have its own user research team, do whatever it takes to make sure those people spend as much time as possible working with your team.

As the well-known computer scientist Frederick Brooks said: “Plan to throw something away anyway. You will have to.”

It may sound a little old-fashioned, and remote collaboration tools keep improving, but the best companies learned an important lesson long ago: bringing a product team together in one place offers enormous advantages.

Or they go to the other extreme, believing they must test and double-check every little detail and, as a result, moving forward rather slowly.

I do not know many people who can do everything this role requires in fewer than sixty hours a week.

Most products inspire nobody, yet human life is too short to spend it using lousy products.

I promised myself that I would never again work so hard and enthusiastically on a product until I knew what users and customers actually wanted.
