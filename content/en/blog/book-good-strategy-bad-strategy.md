The central idea of strategy is to use strengths to overcome weaknesses. Yet most companies focus on goals that are either unrelated or mutually contradictory.

**Strategy** is a long road through difficulties, a way of confronting them, a solution to a complex challenge, and a particular response to a problem.

Developing a good strategy requires leaders who can say a firm “no” to a wide range of actions and interests.

A company may lack a good strategy for two reasons: either it does not need a strategy (which does happen), or its leaders are using a bad one.

**The advantage of a good strategy** is that other companies do not have one and assume you do not either. An effective strategy combines coordinated actions, working methods and resources directed toward a specific result. Many companies replace strategy with numerous goals and initiatives meant to symbolize progress.

A good strategy describes what you want to achieve, why it matters, and how you will do it.

## Symptoms of bad strategy

- **Fluff**. Essentially, empty talk that imitates strategic concepts and reasoning. It tends to rely on fashionable terminology, inflated language and openly esoteric ideas understood only by a select few. All this nonsense creates the illusion of a highly intellectual approach.
- **Failure to identify and address problems**. A bad strategy cannot acknowledge or resolve a problem. If you cannot define the problem, you can never assess the strategy or improve it.
- **Mistaking goals for strategy**. Many bad strategies merely declare a desired outcome instead of planning how to overcome the obstacles to it.
- **Bad strategic objectives**. Leaders set strategic objectives as a means of achieving a particular result. Objectives are bad when they fail to address the most important questions or are simply unattainable.

A strategy must contain at least a “kernel”: a diagnosis, a guiding policy and a set of actions.
