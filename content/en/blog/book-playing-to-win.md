Lately I have been struggling to keep up with my plans. I need to think seriously about how effective my planning systems are and how I use my time. Otherwise, summer will pass me by again :)

Whenever I pick up a book about business development or strategy, I read it as a book about personal development. It works the other way round too. To me, these are equally valid fields, with the same principles and philosophy.

Playing to Win is one of those books: set a clear objective, assess the battlefield, develop a strategy and head toward victory. Strategy is a choice. A company's leadership needs to be able to make decisions. Surprising? Not really. But how to make that choice correctly, based on a clear goal, and on which playing field—that is a more difficult question.

> Making a certain profit is not a strategy. Beating competitors and becoming a leader is not a strategy. Strategy is a way to win. It is a mutually reinforcing set of choices about where to play and who will play on your side, so that you meet customers' needs in a distinctive way and make the business more competitive.

Only one path leads to victory: make a decision and act on it. Winning is the only thing that matters, the main criterion for a successful strategy. You just need to figure out who you intend to win with and whom you intend to beat…

A strategy can be expressed through answers to five key questions:

1. What is your winning aspiration?
2. Where will you play?
3. How will you win?
4. What capabilities must you have?
5. What management systems must you put in place?

Plus a couple of super recommendations: play to win, not just to participate; the winner takes all; and so on, along with plenty of examples of how wonderful P&G is.

The book is about setting big goals—missions—for companies and finding the right way to build a strategy to achieve them.
