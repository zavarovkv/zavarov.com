I will start my Top 500 with this short book. It is more like a handbook of practical advice on the basics of project management, the kind that can be useful to reread occasionally.

Here are some points I learned, remembered or rediscovered. First, the questions to answer when defining a project:

1. Why is this project needed?
2. What will I get as a result of completing it?
3. Will there be any additional outcomes?
4. Which tasks are explicitly outside its scope?
5. Is anything missing? Does it overlap with other projects? Might its boundaries change?
6. Could serious problems arise?
7. Are there any special requirements imposed by the client or the circumstances?

It sounds obvious and simple, but useful. I will refine the list and add some questions of my own.

A project plan is one of the most important elements of project management. In a sense, it is a list of the problems that need to be solved to complete the project. How to build one:

1. Break tasks down into atomic components.
2. Estimate how long each task will take.
3. Define their sequence.
4. Identify the resources needed to carry out the plan.
5. Check which resources are actually available.
6. Evaluate the resulting plan.

When developing a plan, it is useful to define fixed reference points or milestones that must never move. A particularly useful piece of advice for large projects.

Questions worth asking yourself after a project ends:

1. Which practices should I repeat?
2. What will I stop doing?
3. What will I definitely introduce in the next project?
4. Is there anything else I learned that is worth remembering?

Excellent questions. I will put them to use.

My overall impression: a step-by-step guide to project management for beginners, plus a handy reminder for experienced practitioners.
