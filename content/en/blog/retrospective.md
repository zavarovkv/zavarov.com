A retrospective is a form of group reflection — a tool for continuous improvement and cultural change within a team.

Most of us encounter the term "retro" in the context of end-of-sprint ceremonies in iterative development. However, the majority of teams don't follow Scrum or any iterative approach to building IT products. That's no reason to abandon either self-reflection or group reflection when hitting key project milestones.

Below is a detailed 8-step plan with timings for running an engaging retrospective with a large team (10+ people).

**Step 1 — Preparation**

*Time: 30+ minutes*

The more frequently you run retros, the less preparation time you'll need. Your first retrospective might take over an hour or two to prepare; eventually, you'll get it down to under thirty minutes.

Preparation involves creating an event template (for online sessions) in Miro or Figma and adapting it to the specifics of the particular session. Adaptation means modifying, removing, or adding sections and adjusting timings based on the number of participants.

In the basic format described below, the session runs for one and a half hours.

**Step 2 — Opening Remarks**

*Time: 3 minutes*

Start quickly without wasting precious time on formalities — but do cover the goals of the retro and the time period it encompasses. Remind everyone of a few ground rules: staying engaged, being considerate in how you phrase things, and any other logistics that will help the session run smoothly.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-1.webp" alt="Шаг 2 — Вводное слово">

**Step 3 — Setting the Mood**

*Time: 5 minutes*

The goal of this stage is to warm the team up for the serious work ahead. There are plenty of ways to do this — around 30 different options can be found at [retromat.org](https://retromat.org/ru/?id=1-2-3-18-22-31-32-36-42-43-46-52-59-70-76-81-82-84-85-90-106-107-108-114-122-129-130). One simple approach: ask participants to mark their current mood on a board using weather metaphors, as shown in the screenshot below.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-2.webp" alt="Шаг 3 — Создание атмосферы">

**Step 4 — Gathering Information**

*Time: 20 minutes*

Before this stage begins, prepare a board template with a timeline broken into phases, to help participants focus on the specific phases you've chosen — for example, phases of a product or project lifecycle.

The main task for participants: use sticky notes to mark "What went well" and "What got in the way" within the allotted time. It's important to remind everyone beforehand to add enough detail on their stickies, since others will be reading them too.

At the end of this step, quickly run through the green stickies and read out the most notable ones. After that, green stickies won't be the focus anymore.

You can find various approaches to this stage at [retromat.org](https://retromat.org/ru/?id=4-5-6-7-19-33-35-47-51-54-62-64-65-75-78-79-80-86-87-89-93-97-98-110-116-119-121-123-126-127-128-133&phase=1) and pick whichever fits best.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-3.webp" alt="Шаг 4 — Сбор информации">

**Step 5 — Identifying the Most Critical Issues (by vote)**

*Time: 15 minutes*

In this stage, give each participant ***n*** votes to cast on the red stickies that represent the most critical problems — the ones that got in the way most during the project or product work.

Once the dot voting is complete, select the stickies with the most important issues. I recommend picking between 3 and 5.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-5.webp" alt="Шаг 5 — Голосование">

**Step 6 — Generating Solution Hypotheses**

*Time: 20 minutes*

By the start of this stage, the team has identified the top 3–5 problems. Now it's time to think about potential ways to address them. Proposed solutions should be written out in detail on sticky notes — they need to be specific and actionable.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-6.webp" alt="Шаг 6 — Генерирование гипотез">

**Step 7 — Choosing the Best Hypotheses (by vote)**

*Time: 15 minutes*

In this stage, the team selects the best solution for each problem through discussion and/or voting. The chosen solution is recorded on stickies on the right side of the board, and an owner — someone who will try to implement it — is assigned.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-7.webp" alt="Шаг 7 — Выбор лучших гипотез">

**Step 8 — Closing the Retrospective**

*Time: 10 minutes*

One option: have participants write thank-you notes to their teammates (or even to themselves) on one sticky, and on a second sticky write "One thing I will change about how I work" — right after the meeting wraps up.

Various closing techniques are also available at [retromat.org](https://retromat.org/ru/?id=14-15-16-17-23-34-40-44-45-53-57-60-67-71-77-83-92-101-102-104-109-112-120&phase=4).
