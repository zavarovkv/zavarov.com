A retrospective is a group reflection exercise — a tool for continuous improvement and cultural change within a team.

Most of us hear about "retros" in the context of end-of-sprint ceremonies in iterative development. However, the vast majority of teams don't follow Scrum or any iterative approach to building software products. That's no reason to abandon self-reflection or group reflection when you hit key project milestones.

Below is a detailed 8-step plan with timings for running an engaging retrospective with a large team (10+ people).

**Step 1 — Preparation**

*Time: 30+ minutes*

The more frequently you run retros, the less time preparation takes. The first one might take an hour or two; eventually, you'll get it down to under thirty minutes.

Preparation involves creating an event template (for online sessions) in Miro or Figma and adapting it to the specifics of this particular session. Adapting means modifying, removing, or adding blocks and adjusting timings based on the number of participants.

The baseline format described below runs for one and a half hours.

**Step 2 — Opening Remarks**

*Time: 3 minutes*

Get started quickly — don't waste precious time on formalities. That said, you do need to cover the goals of the retro and the time period it spans. Remind everyone of the ground rules: staying engaged, being considerate in how you phrase things, and any other organizational norms you want to maintain to get the most out of the session.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-1.webp" alt="Step 2 — Opening Remarks">

**Step 3 — Setting the Mood**

*Time: 5 minutes*

The goal of this stage is to ease the team into the more focused work ahead. There are many ways to do this — around 30 different options are available at [retromat.org](https://retromat.org/ru/?id=1-2-3-18-22-31-32-36-42-43-46-52-59-70-76-81-82-84-85-90-106-107-108-114-122-129-130). One simple approach: ask participants to mark their current mood on a board using weather metaphors, as shown in the screenshot below.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-2.webp" alt="Step 3 — Setting the Mood">

**Step 4 — Gathering Information**

*Time: 20 minutes*

Before this stage begins, prepare a board template with a timeline broken into phases, to help participants focus on specific periods. These could be, for example, phases of a product or project lifecycle.

The main task for participants: within the allotted time, add sticky notes under "What went well" and "What got in the way." Remind everyone beforehand to be specific on their stickies — other people will be reading them too.

At the end of this step, quickly scan through the green stickies and read out the highlights. After this, the green stickies won't be the focus anymore.

Various formats for this stage can also be found at [retromat.org](https://retromat.org/ru/?id=4-5-6-7-19-33-35-47-51-54-62-64-65-75-78-79-80-86-87-89-93-97-98-110-116-119-121-123-126-127-128-133&phase=1).

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-3.webp" alt="Step 4 — Gathering Information">

**Step 5 — Identifying the Most Critical Issues (by Vote)**

*Time: 15 minutes*

At this stage, give each participant ***n*** votes to place on the red stickies that represent the most critical problems — the ones that got in the way the most during the project or product work.

Once the dot voting is complete, select the stickies with the highest vote counts. I recommend picking between 3 and 5.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-5.webp" alt="Step 5 — Voting">

**Step 6 — Generating Solution Hypotheses**

*Time: 20 minutes*

By the start of this stage, you have the team's top 3–5 problems. Now it's time to think about how to address them. Proposed solutions should be written out in detail on stickies — they need to be concrete and actionable.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-6.webp" alt="Step 6 — Generating Hypotheses">

**Step 7 — Selecting the Best Hypotheses (by Vote)**

*Time: 15 minutes*

At this stage, the team selects the best solution for each problem through discussion or voting. The chosen solution is captured on stickies to the right, and an owner is assigned — someone who will take responsibility for implementing it.

<img loading="lazy" src="/images/retro-step-7.webp" alt="Step 7 — Selecting the Best Hypotheses">

**Step 8 — Closing the Retrospective**

*Time: 10 minutes*

One option: ask participants to write a thank-you note to a teammate (or even to themselves) on one sticky, and on another: "One thing I'll change in how I work" — to act on immediately after the session ends.

Various closing techniques can also be found at [retromat.org](https://retromat.org/ru/?id=14-15-16-17-23-34-40-44-45-53-57-60-67-71-77-83-92-101-102-104-109-112-120&phase=4).
