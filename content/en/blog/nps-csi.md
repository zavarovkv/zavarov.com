<h2>Loyalty</h2>

**Customer Loyalty** is devotion to a brand or product. It means that when faced with comparable alternatives, a user will choose our product. This choice is often emotional — a user who loves a product will recommend it to friends and family.

The standard metric for measuring customer loyalty is the **NPS (Net Promoter Score)**, whose growth positively correlates with sales growth and market share gains. This relationship, combined with how easy the metric is to measure, is exactly what made it so popular worldwide. That said, NPS has its fair share of pitfalls — here's a good article on the topic: [«12 подводных камней Net Promoter Score»](https://hardclient.com/nps-pitfalls).

There is also a well-known study, [«The use of Net Promoter Score (NPS) to predict sales growth: insights from an empirical investigation»](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-021-00790-2), in which a group of researchers derived and quantitatively confirmed a positive correlation between NPS growth and sales.

<h2>Satisfaction</h2>

**Customer Satisfaction** is how well a user's expectations of interacting with a product match their actual experience. An important nuance here is that a regular user may encounter problems at some stages of the CJM while having no issues at others.

For example, imagine you decide to use Yandex Taxi, but the driver is speeding and creating a dangerous situation on the road.

Will you be satisfied with the service at the end of the ride? Probably not — you'll give the driver a low rating, and your satisfaction score will be low. In moments like these, a well-run service will try to gather details about the problem so it can be resolved.

Will your loyalty to the service or product change? Not necessarily. If your previous rides were comfortable, it likely won't. And if you receive a refund or a promo code for your next ride, your loyalty might actually increase. This is a hypothetical example of a situation where satisfaction is low but loyalty remains high.

One of the most widely used metrics for measuring satisfaction is the **CSI (Customer Satisfaction Index)**.

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>

- CSI is a tactical metric; NPS is a strategic one.
- Growth in CSI positively correlates with NPS.
- CSI measures satisfaction at individual stages of the CJM, while NPS reflects the overall perception of the entire product.
- CSI is a rational, evaluative metric; NPS is an emotional one.
- Both CSI and NPS positively correlate with sales.
