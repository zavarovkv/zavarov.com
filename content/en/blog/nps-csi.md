<h2>Loyalty</h2>

**Customer Loyalty** is a user's devotion to a brand or product. It means that, all else being equal, a user will choose our product over the alternatives. This choice is often emotional — a user who loves a product will recommend it to friends and family.

The standard metric for measuring customer loyalty is the **NPS (Net Promoter Score)**, whose growth is positively correlated with sales growth and market share gains. This relationship, combined with the simplicity of measuring the index, is what made it so widely adopted. That said, NPS has its fair share of pitfalls — here's a good article on the subject: [«12 подводных камней Net Promoter Score»](https://hardclient.com/nps-pitfalls).

There is also a notable study, [«The use of Net Promoter Score (NPS) to predict sales growth: insights from an empirical investigation»](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-021-00790-2), in which a group of researchers established and quantitatively confirmed a positive correlation between NPS growth and sales.

<h2>Satisfaction</h2>

**Customer Satisfaction** is the degree to which a user's expectations of interacting with a product match their actual experience. An important nuance here is that a regular user may encounter problems at some stages of the CJM while having no issues at others.

For example, say you decided to use Yandex Taxi, but your driver was speeding recklessly, putting other road users at risk.

Would you be satisfied with the service at the end of that ride? Probably not — you'd give the driver a low rating, and your satisfaction score would be low. In moments like these, a well-run service will try to gather the details of the problem from you so it can be resolved.

Would your loyalty to the service or product change? Not necessarily. If your previous rides were comfortable, it likely wouldn't. And if you received a refund or a promo code for your next trip, your loyalty might even increase. This is a hypothetical example of a situation where satisfaction is low, but loyalty remains high.

One of the most popular metrics for measuring satisfaction is **CSI (Customer Satisfaction Index)**.

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>

- CSI is a tactical metric; NPS is a strategic one.
- Growth in CSI is positively correlated with NPS.
- CSI measures satisfaction at individual stages of the CJM, while NPS captures the overall perception of the product as a whole.
- CSI is a rational, evaluative metric; NPS is an emotional one.
- Both CSI and NPS are positively correlated with sales.
