## On his attitude to life

Above all, I was lucky. I never really had to earn a living. From an economic point of view, working to support yourself seems rather foolish to me. I hope a day will come when people no longer have to work. Thanks to luck, I never had to strain myself. At some point I realized that it was not worth overloading life with excessive responsibilities: a wife, children, a country house, a car… Fortunately, I understood that early enough to enjoy life as a bachelor, taking much more pleasure in it than I would have if I had faced all the usual complications. That is the main thing, really, and it is why I consider myself a very happy man.

## On art

The word “art,” on the other hand, interests me a great deal. I have heard that it comes from Sanskrit and means “making.”

## His first known painting

[Church at Blainville](https://www.wikiart.org/en/marcel-duchamp/church-at-blainville-1902)

## On painting

I lived a little like the bohemians of Montmartre, for whom living, painting and being an artist were taken for granted and meant nothing particularly special. To some extent, it is still like that: you paint simply because you want to be free and avoid going to an office every morning.

A painting that does not shock is not worth the effort put into it.

## On relationships with other people

I said to myself: “Well, if that is the case, there is no point in joining any group. I must rely on myself and act alone.”

Reconciling Art with the People is just a pretty piece of nonsense! I simply did not care about that contact with the public.

## On retinal art

I wanted to avoid getting stuck in a retinal language. Everything was becoming conceptual: it began to depend on things other than the retina.

## On taste

Taste is a habit. The repetition of something once accepted. If you do the same thing several times, it becomes “your taste.” Good or bad, it makes no difference: taste is taste.

## On the death of painting

You see, I believe that painting dies. A painting dies after forty or fifty years because its freshness is gone. Sculpture dies too. That is my little “Dada”; nobody agrees with me, but I do not care. It seems to me that a painting dies after a certain time, just like the person who created it. After that, it becomes part of art history.
