People without a dream are laborers without a goal.

This story reminded me of Daniel Keyes's Flowers for Algernon, about Charlie, a man with an intellectual disability. Here that role belongs to Lennie, a kindhearted man of enormous strength.

Lennie and George are two friends with an idyllic dream: to buy their own ranch, work only for themselves and, at Lennie's insistence, raise rabbits he can stroke.

People who labor on other people's ranches are the loneliest people in the world. They have no family. They have no home. They arrive at a ranch, do their work, then head into town to spend their money. Before long, they are trudging toward another ranch. And there is nothing ahead of them.

That dream brings the friends to another farm to earn some money. All the tragic events take place there…

## Quotations to remember

> “People do not stick together much,” he said thoughtfully. “I do not know why. Maybe everyone in this damned world is afraid of everyone else.”

> People are never satisfied, as the saying goes: give them one thing and they ask for another. It is said disapprovingly, though in fact this is one of humanity's greatest gifts. It is what allowed people to rise above animals, which are always satisfied with what they have.

> People like that have no family. They make a little money, then spend it all. They have no roots or kin, and nobody looks after them…

> Nobody gives charity as generously as a poor person who has suddenly become rich.

> A truly intelligent person is rarely kind.
