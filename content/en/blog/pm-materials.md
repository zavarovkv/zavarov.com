There's no shortage of materials online for developing product skills and broadening your horizons. But I want to bring together the most useful, practical, and valuable ones. This list is continuously improving — new materials get added, outdated ones removed. I hope you find something worth your time.

<h2>Introduction to Product Culture and the Profession</h2>

- [Essay "How to Do Great Work," Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html). An essay by the iconic Paul Graham on how to do your work well — full of advice and reflection. Recommended reading for anyone starting out in their career, not just product managers.
- [Article "Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager," Ben Horowitz](https://a16z.com/2012/06/15/good-product-managerbad-product-manager/). A short piece written in 2012 that remains just as relevant today. It gives a solid general picture of what qualities a good product manager should have.
- [Article "Amazon Leadership Principles," Amazon](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles). Amazon's 14 leadership principles — the standard expected of managers at the company and a mandatory part of their interview process. If they don't resonate with you on the first read, it's worth coming back to them later.

- ["Jedi Techniques," Maxim Dorofeev](https://www.mann-ivanov-ferber.ru/books/dzhedajskie-texniki)
- ["Herding Cats: A Primer for Programmers Who Lead Programmers," J. Hank Rainwater](https://www.litres.ru/book/dzh-hank-reynvoter/kak-pasti-kotov-nastavlenie-dlya-programmistov-rukovody-167876/)
- ["Managing Humans: Biting and Humorous Tales of a Software Engineering Manager," Michael Lopp](https://www.litres.ru/book/maykl-lopp-18021015/kak-upravlyat-intellektualami-ya-nerdy-i-giki-40595322/)

<h2>Research</h2>

- ["The Mom Test," Rob Fitzpatrick](https://www.litres.ru/book/robert-fitcpatrik/sprosi-mamu-kak-obschatsya-s-klientami-i-podtverdit-prav-23963007/). Essential reading on customer discovery interviews. People have strong opinions about the approach in this book — some love it, some push back — but everyone's read it and no one was left indifferent.
- ["Running Lean: Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works," Ash Maurya](https://www.amazon.com/Running-Lean-Iterate-Plan-Works/dp/1098108779/)
- [Video "How to Talk to Users," Eric Migicovsky (Y Combinator)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MT4Ig2uqjTc)
- [Video "ABCDX Segmentation," Artem Azevich (IIDF Accelerator)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LGMfr3he0lc)
- [Video "Market Sizing," Alexander Skurikhin (IIDF Accelerator, Yandex)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPdp_nFW6YY)
- [producthunt.com](https://www.producthunt.com/)

<h2>Analytics</h2>

- ["Playing the Numbers: How Analytics Helps Video Games Thrive," Vasily Sabirov](https://www.litres.ru/vasiliy-sabirov-3241/igra-v-cifry-kak-analitika-pozvolyaet-videoigram-zhit/). Probably the only book on product analytics written by a Russian author. It reads less like a textbook and more like a conversation with a product analyst.
- ["Say It with Charts: The Executive's Guide to Visual Communication," Gene Zelazny](https://www.mann-ivanov-ferber.ru/books/mif/026/)
- [GoPractice Blog](https://gopractice.ru/). Simply an encyclopedia of product analytics and product management knowledge. Exceptionally well-written articles. Best enjoyed slowly, in the evening, over a cup of tea.
- [Free course "Fundamentals of Statistics and A/B Testing" by Yandex Practicum](https://practicum.yandex.ru/statistics-basic/). An excellent introduction to statistics and A/B testing, put together by Practicum's team of mathematicians.
- [Free course "Fundamentals of Statistics" by Anatoly Karpov](https://stepik.org/course/76/syllabus). The most popular statistics course in the Russian-speaking internet, where Anatoly walks through both descriptive and inferential statistics in a clear, structured way. It has become a classic in the Russian-language learning community.

<h2>Strategy</h2>

- ["Good Strategy Bad Strategy," Richard Rumelt](https://www.mann-ivanov-ferber.ru/books/paperbook/good-strategy-bad-strategy/). A foundational book on strategy that explains what good strategy looks like — and what the many bad versions look like. There's a short summary on my blog.
- ["The Goal: A Process of Ongoing Improvement," Eliyahu Goldratt](https://www.litres.ru/book/eliyahu-goldratt/cel-process-nepreryvnogo-sovershenstvovaniya-8648054/)

<h2>Delivery Management / Backlog Management</h2>

- "Getting Real: The Smarter, Faster, Easier Way to Build a Successful Web Application," Jason Fried + David Heinemeier Hansson + Matthew Linderman
- [Article "20 Product Prioritization Techniques: A Map and Guided Tour"](https://foldingburritos.com/blog/product-prioritization-techniques/). A detailed overview of 20 techniques for prioritizing your backlog or hypotheses.
- [Article "RICE: Simple prioritization for product managers," Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers). The original article from Intercom, where this prioritization method was born. It also includes an Excel template you can put to use right away.

- [Podcast "Sobes" (Interview)](https://podcast.ru/1638899174/e)
- [Podcast "Dva Stula" (Two Chairs)](https://www.youtube.com/@dvastulahr)

<h2>Computer Science Basics</h2>

- [Free Python courses "Generation Python"](https://stepik.org/course/58852/syllabus). A series of free Python courses from scratch by professional developer and instructor Timur Guev. Timur comes from a generation of mathematicians who scored perfect marks on Russia's national university entrance exams and a generation of developers who went on to work at FAANG companies. You won't find better beginner courses than this series.
- [Free SQL trainer at sql-ex.ru](https://www.sql-ex.ru/). One of the best — if not the best — SQL practice platforms, and it's free. It has an enormous number of exercises; I recommend working through them to build solid SQL query skills. Don't let the dated interface put you off.
- ["System Design Interview," Alex Xu](https://www.piter.com/product/system-design-podgotovka-k-slozhnomu-intervyu)

<h2>Data Science Basics</h2>

- [Article "Machine Learning for People," vas3k](https://vas3k.blog/blog/machine_learning/). A thorough introduction for anyone who wants to finally make sense of machine learning — written in plain language, without formulas or theorems, with real-world examples and solutions.
- ["Machine Learning for Humans," Vishal Maini, Samir Sabri](https://www.dropbox.com/s/e38nil1dnl7481q/machine_learning.pdf?dl=0)

<h2>Inspiring Stories</h2>

- ["Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future," Ashlee Vance](https://www.litres.ru/book/eshli-vens/ilon-mask-tesla-spacex-i-doroga-v-buduschee-11835578/). The story of Musk's rise as an entrepreneur — the obstacles he overcame, the risks he took. Including his trip to Russia in 2001 and his attempt to buy an intercontinental ballistic missile.
- ["Pour Your Heart Into It: How Starbucks Built a Company One Cup at a Time," Dori Jones Yang, Howard Schultz](https://www.litres.ru/govard-shulc/kak-chashka-za-chashkoy-stroilas-starbucks/)
- ["The Yandex Book," Dmitry Sokolov-Mitrich](https://www.litres.ru/book/dmitriy-sokolov-mitrich/yandeks-kniga-5973039/). The story of Yandex's rise from its origins to its IPO. About the personalities of its founders. About how Sergey Brin and Larry Page came to Moscow in 2003 to acquire the company, but Arkady Volozh and Ilya Segalovich refused to sell — and went on to build the most technologically advanced IT company in Russia.
