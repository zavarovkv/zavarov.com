I am falling well behind the goal I set myself: I need to read twice as much. Before reading Drucker, I did not know how to do that while maintaining or even improving my effectiveness. The picture is clearer now, but more on that another time…

I accumulated an enormous number of notes. This is a book about effectiveness, why it matters, how it relates to time, and how to become more effective. An organization's success depends on the effectiveness of its executives. A person's success depends on carrying out plans effectively. We can always manage ourselves, so each of us is the executive of at least one person.

Without effectiveness, high performance is impossible, however much knowledge, time and effort we put into the work.

An amusing example about responsibility and decisions stayed with me:

> “I alone am responsible here. If my men encounter the enemy in the jungle and do not know what to do, I will be too far away to help. My job is to make sure they know how to act. Their choice will depend on the situation, which only they can assess at that moment. I carry the responsibility, but the people on the ground make the decisions.”

A person's work will be ineffective unless they make a conscious effort to make it effective. To work effectively, they also need to understand related fields. One weakness of today's educated young people, whether in business, medicine or government, is that they limit themselves to excellent knowledge of one narrow area while looking down on other disciplines…

What effective executives have in common is an ability to achieve positive results in everything they undertake. Effectiveness is a habit.

There are five basic practices, “five habits,” that executives must develop to work effectively:

1. **Effective executives know where their time goes.** They work systematically to manage the small portion of their time they can actually control.
2. They concentrate on contributions beyond their organizations. **Their focus is on the final result, not on doing work for its own sake.**
3. They build on strengths: their own, their bosses', their colleagues' and their subordinates'. **In difficult situations, they rely on strengths rather than fixating on weaknesses.**
4. They concentrate on a few major areas where excellent work will produce outstanding results. They know they have no choice but to **do first things first and never do second things.**
5. Effective executives make effective decisions. **They know that effective decisions are judgments based on differing opinions.**

The first and most important piece of advice in the book is to find out where your time goes. Plans and planning take second place :) You cannot manage your time if you do not know how you spend it. You also need to identify the things that should not be done at all and stop doing them.

> Time is the scarcest resource. If you cannot manage it, you cannot manage anything.

Sister Bryan's rule: ask yourself every day whether you have made the greatest possible contribution to solving the problem or achieving the goal.

On setting great goals:

> In many cases, people who did not succeed worked much harder. Yet they did not set themselves more demanding tasks and ignored the need to redirect their efforts.

Four basic requirements for effective interpersonal relationships:

1. Communication.
2. Teamwork.
3. Self-development.
4. Developing others.

As for meetings, I learned long ago to arrive prepared, knowing clearly what I expect and carrying a specific list of questions. There are other rules that make meetings more productive too: you can either lead the meeting and listen to participants or take part in the discussion yourself. You cannot combine the two. Above all, you need to focus participants on a specific contribution from the outset.

The next point helped me understand some people I work with. They are strong individuals who do not need an organization and do not want to become part of one. They prefer to act independently.

A few questions I should ask myself to assess an employee's strengths:

1. What do they do well?
2. What else are they likely to do well, building on their abilities?
3. What do they need to learn to make full use of their strengths?
4. If I had a son or daughter, would I want them to work for this person in the future? Why or why not?

Something to put into practice.

A few more ideas that stayed with me, as quotations because I do not have time to expand on them :)

> If there is one main secret of effectiveness, it is concentration.
>
> Systematically getting rid of the old is the only way to start something new.
>
> Today's situation is always the result of yesterday's actions and decisions.

Effectiveness can be learned. An excellent book, with plenty to put into practice right now. A must-read.
